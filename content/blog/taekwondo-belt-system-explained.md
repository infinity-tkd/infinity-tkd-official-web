Belts are the most visible part of Taekwondo, but the system behind them is more than a colour chart. Each rank marks a stage of technical knowledge, physical development and character. Here is how the ladder works.

## Two families of rank: geup and dan

Taekwondo ranks are split into two groups:

- **Geup (also written *gup*)** are the "student" ranks, counted **downwards** from 10th geup (beginner) to 1st geup (the final stage before black belt).
- **Dan** are the "degree" ranks, counted **upwards** from 1st dan (first-degree black belt) to 9th dan.

This is why a 1st geup is *higher* than a 5th geup, while a 5th dan is *higher* than a 1st dan.

## What the colours traditionally mean

Colour sequences differ slightly between schools, and many schools place two geup ranks in each colour band (for example, a solid belt followed by the same colour with a stripe). A widely used progression and its traditional symbolism:

| Belt | Traditional meaning |
| --- | --- |
| White | Purity and a fresh start; no prior knowledge |
| Yellow | Earth, from which a seed begins to take root |
| Green | Growth, as the plant rises and strengthens |
| Blue | The sky, toward which the plant reaches |
| Brown | Ripening and solidity; a firm foundation |
| Red | Danger and power; the need for self-control |
| Black | Maturity and mastery; also the beginning of deeper study |

These meanings are traditional teaching aids rather than strict rules, so you may hear slightly different versions at different schools.

At **Infinity Taekwondo** the ladder has eight ranks: White, Yellow, Green, Blue, Brown and Red, then Black Belt 1st Dan and 2nd Dan. Each colour belt adds new Taegeuk forms, Red belt learns Koryo, and the black belts learn the remaining black belt forms plus New and Freestyle Poomsae. ## Black belt is a beginning, not an ending

Reaching 1st dan is a major achievement — and it is also the point at which students begin to study the art more deeply. Higher dan ranks bring more advanced patterns, teaching responsibility and, over time, years of service to the Taekwondo community.

Internationally recognised Dan certificates are issued by **Kukkiwon**, the world headquarters of Taekwondo, which is why many schools align their curriculum with its standards.

## Poom ranks for younger students

Younger students who pass a black belt level test usually receive a **Poom** rank rather than a Dan rank. Poom holders typically wear a distinct belt and convert to the equivalent Dan rank once they reach the age set by the governing body. The training is the same; only the title differs until then.

## How grading works

Grading (or belt testing) is a structured assessment, usually held several times a year. A typical test includes:

1. **Basic techniques** — stances, blocks, punches and kicks.
2. **Poomsae** — the pattern required for your level.
3. **Demonstration, tricking and self-defense (hosinsul)** skills, as appropriate to rank and age.
4. **Breaking** (board breaking), with more boards and harder techniques as you progress.
5. **Knowledge** — terminology, rules and etiquette.

Your instructor decides when you are ready to test. They look at technique, attendance, attitude and understanding — not only how well you perform on the day.

## Tips for steady progress

- **Train consistently.** Two or three sessions a week beats one long session followed by a gap.
- **Practise your poomsae at home,** even for ten minutes.
- **Ask questions.** Understanding *why* a movement works makes it easier to remember.
- **Be patient with plateaus.** Everyone has them; they usually come just before a breakthrough.
