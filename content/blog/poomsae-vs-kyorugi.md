Ask ten Taekwondo students what they enjoy most and you will hear two very different answers: some love the precision and flow of **poomsae**, others live for the pace and tactics of **kyorugi**. They look different, but they are two halves of the same art.

## Poomsae: patterns of precision

Poomsae are pre-arranged sequences of stances, blocks, strikes and kicks performed against imaginary opponents. They are the "grammar" of Taekwondo — a way of rehearsing technique, balance, rhythm and breath until movements become second nature.

**What it trains:**

- Accurate stances and weight transfer
- Control, balance and body awareness
- Focus, memory and calm under pressure
- The correct application and timing of each technique

### Recognised and freestyle poomsae

In World Taekwondo competition there are two main formats:

- **Recognised poomsae** — the official forms. Colour-belt students practise the eight **Taegeuk** forms; black belts progress through forms such as **Koryo**, **Keumgang** and beyond.
- **Freestyle poomsae** — creative routines set to music that combine kicking, acrobatics and choreography within defined rules.

### How recognised poomsae is scored

Competition scoring rewards both accuracy and expression. A performance is judged out of 10.0:

| Component | Maximum | What judges look at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | 4.0 | Correct stances, techniques and sequence; points are deducted for errors |
| Presentation | 6.0 | Speed and power, rhythm and tempo, and expression of energy |

## Kyorugi: sparring with strategy

Kyorugi is free sparring between two athletes. It is fast, tactical and physical — and it develops reflexes, timing, distance control and resilience.

**What it trains:**

- Timing and distance management
- Footwork and fast, accurate kicking
- Tactical decision-making
- Composure under pressure

### The competition format

In World Taekwondo competition, athletes wear protective equipment, including a trunk protector, head guard and mouthguard. Matches are contested over **three rounds of two minutes** with short rest periods, and points are awarded for valid kicks and punches to legal scoring areas. Modern events use **electronic scoring** so that valid contact is recorded consistently.

Sparring in the dojang is always supervised and graded to ability — beginners start with controlled drills long before full-contact work.

## Which one should you choose?

You do not have to choose. The two disciplines complement each other:

- **Poomsae sharpens the technique** that makes your sparring kicks cleaner and more powerful.
- **Kyorugi tests that technique** against a moving, resisting partner, which in turn sharpens your poomsae.

Some students lean toward one — that is fine. A strong foundation in both makes you a more complete martial artist, and many athletes discover a passion for the one they expected to like least.
